§ 01

The property, in plain English — what you are actually buying

Sources · Baubeschreibung · Grundbuch · Teilungserklärung · Energieausweis

You are looking at one apartment inside a 14-storey concrete high-rise from 1971, built by Hausbau Wüstenrot in the Eglosheim district of Ludwigsburg (Stuttgart commuter belt). The block contains 48 apartments across multiple sizes — 2½, 3½ and 4½ rooms. Heating and hot water come from Fernwärme (district heating). There are two passenger lifts, a basement storage cellar for each flat, and a separately-organised underground garage.

The seller is not a single private owner — it is the community of heirs (Erbengemeinschaft) of Mr. Georg Bodner, who died in August 2024. His widow Sigrid is in a nursing home, and her three daughters (Renate Schugt, Dagmar Buck, Martina Bodner) inherited his half-share. Any sale needs every heir's signature plus the consent of the WEG administrator (per §12 WEG — though that restriction was lifted by owner resolution in 2008, so administrator consent is in practice not required).

The package includes parking space No. 44 in the underground garage (a separate WEG, Pleidelsheimer Str. 11). The garage is currently rented out to a third party (Mr. Truschinski) for €50/month on an indefinite tenancy that automatically ends "on sale" — so as the buyer you inherit a tenant you will need to terminate.

"For sale (Veräußerung) of the residential ownership, the consent of the administrator is required" — this restriction was lifted in 2008. Good. One less veto.

Which apartment exactly?

Documents are not perfectly consistent. The Grundbuch (sheet 10799) places the unit on the 8th upper floor, left, with a 26/1000 co-ownership share (MEA) — pointing to one of the larger ≈98 m² 4½-room units. The Wirtschaftsplan 2026 confirms the 26/1000 MEA. However, a separate Wüstenrot reserve letter labels owner-no. 1003201 as "8th floor, right, 20/1000" — that conflicts. Get this nailed down before signing (see §02 flag 09).

Short German glossary

WEG
The condominium owners' association — every flat owner is automatically a member.
Teilungserklärung
The "constitution" of the building: how it is divided into units, what each owner owns, who pays what.
Grundbuch
Land register — the definitive record of ownership, mortgages and easements.
MEA (Miteigentumsanteil)
Your slice of the whole building, in thousandths. Most cost shares scale by this.
Hausgeld
Monthly service charge to the WEG — running costs + reserve contribution.
Erhaltungsrücklage
The collective maintenance reserve. The shared piggy bank for the roof, lifts, façade.
Sonderumlage
A one-off special levy when the reserve is not enough. What you fear.
Wirtschaftsplan
The WEG's annual budget — sets your monthly advance payment.
Eigentümerversammlung
The annual owners' meeting; resolutions are binding.
Erbengemeinschaft
Community of heirs — every heir must sign together.
Energieausweis
Energy certificate (mandatory at sale; must be shown to buyer).
Grundschuld
Land charge — like a mortgage entry, even after the loan is paid it stays until cancelled.
§ 02

Findings & red flags — what the documents try not to shout

12 findings · severity-coded
Critical

01 · Active lawsuit against the roofer (GT Bedachung)

The WEG is suing the roofing contractor "GT Bedachungen" before the Regional Court (LG Stuttgart). It first surfaces in the 2023 minutes and is still listed as "open" in the November 2025 minutes — meaning the roof has had problems serious enough to litigate, and the outcome is not yet known.

Why it mattersThis is a flat roof (warm roof, gravel ballast) on a 14-storey block. Roof failures are six-figure events. If the WEG loses or only partially recovers, expect a Sonderumlage (special levy) — and you would inherit your 26/1000 share of it.

Critical

02 · 1971 construction — asbestos-cement drainage piping in the spec

The original Baubeschreibung (construction specification, signed 8.10.1971) lists the roof drains and internal drainage piping as asbestos-cement ("Dachabläufe mit innenliegender Regenwasserführung in Asbestzement"). This was standard for the era but is now a known liability.

Why it mattersAsbestos-cement pipework is generally safe while undisturbed, but any plumbing renovation, vertical-stack repair, or building modernisation triggers special handling, disposal and worker-protection costs. Ask whether stacks have been replaced.

Critical

03 · Owners refused to discharge the management two years running

At the 2024 meeting the owners rejected discharge of Wüstenrot (the long-time manager) for FY2023 by 23–2. They also rejected the 2023 demands for back-payments (23–3) and refused to reappoint Wüstenrot (23–2). A brand-new administrator (Die Immobilienverwalter GmbH) took over on 1 Jan 2025 — only one year of track record so far.

Why it matters"Refusal of discharge" is the WEG's way of saying we don't trust the books. Combined with the 2024 statement only being finalised in October 2025 (very late), there has been administrative turbulence. The new manager seems competent (November 2025 minutes are clean and unanimous) but the underlying disputes about the 2023 numbers may not be resolved.

04 · Maintenance reserve is thin for a 1971 high-rise

End-of-year reserve balance (31.12.2023): €147,892 across 48 apartments = ~€3,080 per unit. The 2026 annual allocation is €42,024/year — about €1,093 per flat at your MEA.

Why it mattersA 1971 high-rise with a flat roof, two lifts, a district-heating transfer station, an emergency power generator and an RWA smoke-extraction system has 6-figure capital items waiting in line (lift modernisation alone is €80–150k per shaft; façade refurbishment can be €500k+). €3k per unit is a cushion, not a war chest. Any of the items in §03 lands as a special levy.

05 · Energy certificate is consumption-based and expires Sept 2027

The certificate (Minol, 18.09.2017) is a Verbrauchsausweis (consumption-based), not a demand-based one. Final-energy value 81 kWh/m²·a, primary 106. Modernisation recommendations: check heating system, check roof insulation, check external walls, windows, basement ceiling — i.e. everything, but no checkboxes are ticked.

Why it mattersThe certificate expires before you could realistically rent out or resell — the seller has a duty to provide a valid one at sale, but a new one will likely come in worse under the post-2024 GEG (Building Energy Act) rules. Federal climate-law trajectory (mandatory thermal upgrades, heating-system replacement, façade insulation) creates a clear medium-term capex risk for the WEG.

§ 05

Questions for the broker — grouped for one conversation

Walk in with this list · do not improvise

A · Legal & ownership

  1. Can you reconcile the discrepancy between the Grundbuch (8th floor LEFT, 26/1000) and the 2025 Wüstenrot reserve letter (8th floor RIGHT, 20/1000)? Which unit am I actually buying?If they can't answer on the spot, do not progress to offer.
  2. Have all three heirs (Renate Schugt, Dagmar Buck, Martina Bodner) and Sigrid Bodner formally agreed to the sale, and at this price? Who signs at the notary?
  3. Will the seller arrange the cancellation of the 1972 Wüstenrot Grundschuld (€40,903.35) before, or at, completion — and bear the cost?
  4. Are there any rights of first refusal (Vorkaufsrechte) — by the city, the tenant, or other owners?
  5. The garage rental to Mr. Truschinski ends "on sale". Will the seller serve the termination notice now, so that I receive a vacant parking space at handover?
  6. Is the apartment itself currently rented, or vacant? Provide the most recent utility bills (Strom, Wasser, Heizkostenabrechnung) to verify.

B · Financial & community

  1. Are there any planned Sonderumlagen (special levies) resolved or under discussion for 2026–2028?
  2. What is the current monetary value at stake in the GT Bedachung lawsuit, and what is counsel's assessment of the WEG's exposure if it loses?
  3. Why did the owners refuse to discharge Wüstenrot for FY2023 — and is that dispute now resolved, or still live?
  4. Will I receive the existing annual statements (Jahresabrechnungen) for 2022, 2023, 2024 in full?
  5. What was the cumulative spend from the reserve over the last 5 years, and on what?
  6. What are the manager's plans for raising the reserve allocation, given the building's age?
  7. Are there any Hausgeld arrears outstanding on this unit at the proposed completion date?

C · Structural & technical

  1. When was the flat roof last refurbished, and what is the warranty status — is the GT Bedachung work still under guarantee?
  2. Have any of the original asbestos-cement drainage stacks been replaced? Where in the building, and at what cost?
  3. When were the two lifts last fully modernised, and what is the current TÜV status?
  4. When was the façade (vorgehängte Fassade, precast concrete elements) last inspected for de-bonding or spalling?
  5. What is the state of the apartment's electrics — original 1971 wiring, or rewired? Fuse box modernised? RCD/FI protection?
  6. What is the condition of the apartment's windows — still the original wooden composite (Holzverbund) windows, or replaced? U-value if known?
  7. Is the bathroom layout original (linoleum + tile-on-screed) — i.e. does the €20–30k renovation budget realistically cover bathroom + kitchen + floors?
  8. Has there ever been water damage in this unit (especially from the roof — given the lawsuit, or from a stack above)?

D · Compliance & future cost

  1. A new Energieausweis will need to be issued before sale — will the seller provide it, and what does the latest building energy consumption look like (last 3 years)?
  2. What is the WEG's plan for compliance with the GEG 2024 / Heizungsgesetz — is the district-heating supplier on a decarbonisation path?
  3. Achievable internet speed at the unit on the current Vodafone connection — and could I install an individual fibre line if I pay myself?
  4. Is the building covered by any heritage protection (Denkmalschutz) or facade-design constraints that would limit renovations or AC installation?

Three things not to do

  • Do notcommit before the unit identity (left vs. right, 20 vs. 26 MEA) is reconciled in writing. This is the single biggest contract risk.
  • Do notaccept a purchase contract that does not explicitly transfer the seller's share of the maintenance reserve to you at completion (under §10 (7) WEG / new WEMoG it stays with the unit, but the contract should reflect it).
  • Do notlet the broker pressure you with "another buyer is interested". Six-figure decisions deserve seven days of reflection. The Erbengemeinschaft cannot easily find another buyer at this price either — time is on your side.

If the seller refuses to move below €285k, walk away. The numbers stop working — and the documents tell you why.
